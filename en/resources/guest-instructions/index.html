Guest instructions for your apartment: what to send before arrival

One well-written pre-arrival message replaces ten calls and midnight messages. Here's what it should contain, when to send it, and a ready-made template you can copy.

SmartStay team · Published 6 November 2026 · 6 min read

In short
  • Before arrival, guests need to know six things: the exact address, how to get there, where to park, how to get in, the Wi-Fi and who to call.
  • Send the instructions 1–2 days before arrival, and a short reminder on the day.
  • A photo of the entrance and a pin on the map save more time than any description.
  • Below is a ready-made message template in Bosnian and English.

What guests need to know before arrival

A guest arriving in an unfamiliar city for the first time, often tired and with luggage, doesn't need a novel. They need answers to the questions they would otherwise ask you by phone:

  • Exact address and a pin on the map. Street and number aren't always enough. In many parts of Sarajevo and other cities, navigation leads to the wrong entrance.
  • How to get there. From the airport, bus or train station: taxi, bus, approximate travel time.
  • Parking. Whether the apartment has parking, where it is, whether it's paid and how. If not, where the nearest public garage is.
  • Entrance and keys. Which building, which entrance, floor, apartment number. Whether you meet them or it's self check-in.
  • Wi-Fi. Network name and password. This is question number one after walking in.
  • Contact. The phone number and channel (WhatsApp, Viber) you answer on.

You can send the house rules in the same message or as a separate link. There's a ready-made one in our house rules template.

When to send the instructions

Too early and guests forget them. Too late and they're already on their way without the information. The schedule that has worked best:

  1. Right after bookingA short thank-you and confirmation. Ask for the approximate arrival time.
  2. 1–2 days before arrivalComplete instructions: address, getting there, parking, entrance, Wi-Fi, contact, house rules.
  3. On the day of arrivalA short reminder: “See you today, here's the link with the instructions.”
  4. The first morning of the stay“Is everything all right?” A small problem solved immediately doesn't end up in a review.

Tips to make the instructions really clear

1. A photo is worth more than a description

A photo of the building entrance, the apartment door and the key box solves most problems before they happen. A guest in the dark, facing three similar entrances, won't read a description — they'll look for the door from the picture.

2. Write from the guest's point of view

Instead of

The apartment is in the building by the bakery.

Write

When you get out of the taxi you'll see a bakery. The building entrance is the green door to the right of it.

3. Everything in one place

When the information is scattered across five messages in the Airbnb chat, guests can't find the Wi-Fi password when they need it. Send everything in one message or, better still, as a single link guests can open at any time.

4. In the guest's language

A guest from Germany or Turkey understands English, but reads instructions in their own language faster and with fewer mistakes, especially when tired after the journey.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to send the instructions as a message or a PDF?

A message or a link beats a PDF. PDFs are awkward to open on a phone, hard to search, and guests have to dig them out of their downloads. A link that opens in the browser is found in a second.

Can I send the entry code right after booking?

Better not. Send the door or key box code only a day or two before arrival, once the booking is definitely confirmed and paid. That reduces the risk of the code circulating long before the stay.

What if the guest doesn't read the instructions?

It happens. That's why you send a short reminder on the day of arrival with a link to the instructions, and put the answer to the most common question (Wi-Fi, entrance) at the very top of the message.

How long should the message with instructions be?

Long enough for guests to see everything important on their phone without much scrolling. The six blocks from the template are enough. Put appliance details and city recommendations in a separate guide.
